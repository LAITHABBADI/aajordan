About Alcoholics Anonymous in Irbid

Irbid is home to one of Jordan's largest universities and a diverse, growing population. Alcohol dependency can affect anyone, and AA provides a path to recovery that is accessible regardless of background, nationality, or religion. Alcoholics Anonymous is not a religious organization — it is a spiritual fellowship that respects the beliefs of all members.

AA meetings in Irbid follow the same format used by tens of thousands of AA groups worldwide. Members gather to share their experience, strength, and hope with one another. The only requirement to attend is a desire to stop drinking. You do not need to have hit rock bottom, lost your job, or faced legal problems — if alcohol is causing problems in your life, AA is for you.

The AA program centers on the Twelve Steps — a framework for addressing the physical, mental, and spiritual dimensions of alcoholism. Many members in Irbid work through the steps with a sponsor, a more experienced AA member who provides personal guidance and support.

If traveling to an in-person meeting is difficult, online AA meetings are available as a convenient alternative. These virtual meetings follow the same 12-step format and connect you with AA members from across Jordan and around the world.

What to Expect at an AA Meeting in Irbid

  • No cost, ever — AA meetings are completely free. We do not charge fees, sell products, or ask for donations from newcomers.
  • Your privacy is protected — AA traditions require that all members maintain strict anonymity. What is shared in the room stays in the room.
  • No obligation to speak — newcomers may simply listen. There is no pressure to share your story until you feel ready.
  • You only need one thing — a desire to stop drinking. That is the only requirement for membership in AA.
  • A welcoming community — members in Irbid come from many backgrounds. You will be met with understanding, not judgment.
  • Practical support — after the meeting, members can connect you with a sponsor and explain how the 12-step program works.

How the AA 12-Step Program Works

The Twelve Steps of Alcoholics Anonymous were first published in 1939 in the book "Alcoholics Anonymous" (commonly called the Big Book). Millions of people worldwide have used the 12 steps to achieve lasting sobriety from alcohol. The steps are not a religious requirement — they are a practical guide for changing the behaviors and attitudes that fuel addictive drinking.

The process begins with admitting powerlessness over alcohol, moves through a personal inventory of past behaviors, and culminates in a commitment to help others in recovery. Members who work all Twelve Steps consistently report long-term sobriety, improved relationships, and a renewed sense of purpose.
